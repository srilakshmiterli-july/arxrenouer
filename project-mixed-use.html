Course: Building Components and Systems | UC Berkeley

Role: Student Designer

Location: Hayes Valley, San Francisco

Concept

Suzu is a multi-level mixed-use interior environment in San Francisco’s Hayes Valley, combining a ground-floor contemporary Japanese restaurant for celebrity chef Makoto Okuwa with a private residential apartment, rooftop amenities, and a community library. The restaurant concept responds to Okuwa’s design priorities — tradition balanced with modern vibrancy, minimal and intentional spaces, and an elegant environment centered around the art of sushi, with an omakase counter, bar, and mezzanine dining.

Program

Restaurant & Bar → Mezzanine Dining → Residential Loft → Rooftop Amenity

Interior & Materials

The bar features a fluted dark walnut face, black marble countertop, brass soffit with walnut ceiling slats, and a teal feature wall as focal backdrop. The double-height dining room uses manufactured stone veneer walls with an open staircase to the mezzanine. The residential apartment layers Acupanel natural oak acoustic slat panels with hardwood flooring and contemporary furnishings in a warm, minimalist palette.

Design Approach

This semester-long project built a complete understanding of how building components and systems work together in a real mixed-use context. Each sequential assignment — from client research and precedent study through floor plans, facade materials, reflected ceiling plans, interior finishes, furniture selections, bar design, lighting, and HVAC coordination — added a layer to a single cohesive design.
