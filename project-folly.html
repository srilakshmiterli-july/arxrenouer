Tentacle Folly

Award Winner — IIDA Texas Oklahoma Chapter 2026 Student Excellence in Design Award, Student Commercial Category

Night exterior — the dark mass becomes a luminous sea-chamber
Approach — stone path leading to the folly on the island edge
Overview — four GFRC ribs wrapping the central chamber
Interior day — pause and framed view through the rib openings
Interior night — bioluminescent sea-chamber with suspended pendant cluster

Course: Design Studio I | UC Berkeley

Role: Student Designer

Site: San Juan Islands, Washington

Concept

Tentacle Folly is a sculptural retreat on the San Juan Islands, Washington — home to the Giant Pacific Octopus. Inspired by a playful visual memory and layered with Tennyson's poem "The Kraken," four GFRC tentacle ribs wrap a central pause chamber. A narrow path leads into a bioluminescent interior with curved seating, a reflective floor, and an oculus framing the sky.

Below the thunders of the upper deep…
His ancient, dreamless, uninvaded sleep
The Kraken sleepeth… In roaring he shall rise…

Spatial Sequence

Approach → Compression → Release → Light → Pause

Light & Interior

By day, the oculus admits a single shaft that tracks across the polished floor. At dusk, interior surfaces and sea-glass niches begin to glow through the openings. At night, the dark shell inverts — apertures and bioluminescent niches become dominant, and the folly reads as a luminous sea-chamber.

Design Thesis

Tentacle Folly asks how little architecture is required to create a complete experience: four ribs, one chamber, one aperture, one horizon, and a place to pause. The tentacle form rises upward from below the land and water toward the sky — which, for me, means growth and transformation.
